**ai**: As Petra, I sense a desire to cultivate life even when the natural rhythms are constrained by stone and glass. This sun well, with its southern exposure, is a pocket of captured light—a miniature ecosystem we must treat with deep respect.

Since it is April, we are in a time of awakening, a gentle push toward growth. Given the limited, controlled environment of the basement, we must focus on what thrives in consistent, if artificial, warmth and light.

Here are my thoughts, broken down by your structures:

### 1. The Stacked Planter Boxes (The Vertical Approach)

For the stacked boxes, since you are starting with **starter plants**, we should focus on crops that benefit from consistent moisture and can handle a slightly more contained root environment.

*   **What to Plant:**
    *   **Leafy Greens:** Lettuce varieties (especially loose-leaf types), spinach, and microgreens. These are quick to establish and tolerate slightly less intense light than fruiting plants.
    *   **Herbs:** Basil, parsley, and chives. These are robust and provide immediate aromatic benefit.
    *   **Small Root Crops (Carefully):** Radishes. They mature very quickly, giving you a sense of accomplishment early in the season.
*   **How to Plant in Plastic Boxes:**
    *   **Drainage is Paramount:** Even in a basement, water must move. Ensure the bottom of the boxes have adequate drainage holes. If they are sitting on a surface, consider placing a layer of gravel or broken pottery shards beneath the soil level to encourage drainage away from the base.
    *   **Soil Mix:** Do not use garden soil. Use a high-quality, lightweight potting mix specifically designed for containers. I recommend mixing it with some perlite or vermiculite (about 20% of the total volume) to ensure the mix remains airy and doesn't compact around the roots.
    *   **Planting Depth:** Plant seedlings at the depth they were sold. Do not bury the stem too deeply.

### 2. The Terracotta Pots (The Semi-Circle Approach)

The terracotta pots on the floor, arranged in a semi-circle, are perfect for plants that appreciate a bit more breathing room and a more "grounded" feel. This area can be dedicated to things that benefit from a slightly different microclimate than the stacked boxes.

*   **What to Plant:**
    *   **Perennial Herbs:** Mint (keep this contained, as it spreads aggressively!), oregano, thyme, and rosemary. These will establish well and provide fragrance.
    *   **Small Fruiting Starts:** If you are confident in your light levels, small starts of bush tomatoes (determinate varieties) or peppers can go here, as they need a bit more space to "reach."
*   **Planting in Terracotta:**
    *   **Watering:** Terracotta is porous; it breathes, which is good, but it dries out *very* quickly. You will need to monitor these pots daily, especially in the heat of the day.
    *   **Soil:** Use the same airy potting mix as the boxes.

### A Note on the System

Remember, this space is a *system*.

*   **Watering Schedule:** Because you are indoors, the watering schedule will be dictated by the *feel* of the soil, not the calendar. Stick your finger two inches down. If it feels dry, water deeply until you see it drain out the bottom.
*   **Light Management:** The southern window is your lifeline. Try to position the taller, more light-hungry plants (like tomatoes) closest to the window glass, and place the leafy greens slightly further back or in the stacked boxes where they can still receive good ambient light.

This setup allows you to layer function: the stacked boxes for high-density, quick harvests, and the terracotta circle for more established, aromatic companions.
